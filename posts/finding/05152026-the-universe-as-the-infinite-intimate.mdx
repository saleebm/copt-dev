I spent my morning commute listening to a YouTube video that I normally would have scrolled right past. The algorithm surfaced an Aubrey Marcus episode with Marc Gafni titled "The Reason Your Prayers Don't Work". My usual media diet consists of system architecture talks and release notes, so clicking on a two-hour discussion about the divine felt like a deliberate break in my routine. What actually caught my attention were the video's chapter markers. Seeing timestamps for "The Problem of Evil and Theodicy" right next to "Prayer as Protest" signaled a heavier, more structural breakdown of belief than the usual self-help platitudes. Gafni frames the concept of a higher power as the "Infinite Intimate"—a conversational presence rather than a distant, unapproachable monolith. I am used to thinking of the universe as a set of cold, executable rules. You write a script, it runs. You make an error, it fails. But the discussion challenged the idea of a passive existence, asking if the universe is actually "open for conversation". The guests argued that spiritual connection isn't just dropping a request into a void; it is an active, ongoing dialogue. I haven't suddenly abandoned my logic-first worldview. Still, there is something structurally elegant about viewing our daily friction—the bugs, the delays, the quiet frustrations—as a two-way street instead of a silent terminal. It reframes the struggle. Maybe we aren't just shouting into the void; maybe we are participating in a much larger feedback loop.
