I spent part of my Friday morning listening to a recent Aubrey Marcus podcast episode featuring Dr. Marc Gafni. They spend nearly two hours deconstructing why people feel disconnected from their own spirituality. Gafni introduces this concept of the divine as the "Infinite Intimate". I am usually skeptical of sprawling, esoteric YouTube podcasts, but that specific phrase caught my attention. We generally default to viewing a higher power as a distant auditor tallying our mistakes. Gafni flips that, suggesting the universe is actually open for conversation. Looking through the video's chapter markers, the one that made me pause was a segment on "Prayer as Protest". It completely reframes the act of praying. Instead of a quiet, submissive plea, it becomes dynamic friction. A viewer in the comments nailed the vibe, noting that prayer is not submission but relationship. I don't know exactly where I sit on the theological spectrum these days, but I appreciate this concrete shift in perspective. Moving from passive obedience to active participation feels like a much more useful way to approach the unknown. It turns the 3am mystery of being alive into a dialogue rather than a monologue.
