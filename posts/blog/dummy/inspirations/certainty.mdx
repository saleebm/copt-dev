# Certainty
> Anything that admits the slightest doubts I will set aside as if I have found it to be wholly false, and I will proceed in this way until I recognize something certain. -- Descartes, *Second Meditation*

//Certainly, after awaiting an eternity, through clear, distinct, pathologically *hope*ful, cocksure, and pure methodical doubt we can each and all know that: (1) half a dull loaf is better than no bread, (2) nothing is certain except death and taxes, except, but ofc, the possibility of cryonics and creative accounting, and (3) certainty is certainly an epistemic error in all possible cases. Under closure, belief and doubt define each other to some *dok*. *Faith* is our plight, or so I have *faith*. Insofar as *The Other* as the Reality of the Ideal can be *named*: the necessarily incoherent, inconsistent, immutable mutability of the incompletion of the closure, *The Dialectic*al *End* of the *Dialetheia*, awaits us all. So, pick your Bobbing turtles wisely.//

> Anything that admits the slightest doubts I will set aside as if I have found it to be wholly false, and I will proceed in this way until I recognize something certain.

> -- Descartes, *Second Meditation*

> What exactly is [Descartes] thinking? Perhaps this: 'The senses sometimes deceive us. So for all we know, they always deceive us'.  But this is a bad argument – a fallacy. Compare: 'Some banknotes are forgeries. So for all we know, they all are forgeries'. Here, the conclusion is impossible, since the very notion of a forgery presupposes valid notes...we only know that the senses sometimes deceive us because further investigation - using the same senses – show that they have done so.
> 
> -- Simon Blackburn, *Think*

> There is no such thing as absolute certainty, but there is assurance sufficient for the purposes of human life.
> 
> -- John Stuart Mill, *On Liberty*

> Doubt is not a pleasant condition, but certainty is absurd. 
> 
> -- Voltaire
